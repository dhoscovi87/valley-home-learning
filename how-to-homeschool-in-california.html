How to legally start homeschooling in California (2026–27)

California has no separate "homeschool" law. The most common route is for your home to become a small private school, which you register by filing a Private School Affidavit (PSA). Other legal routes include a public charter or independent-study program, or a private school satellite program (PSP).

Who has to file?

The compulsory education ages are 6 to 18. A parent teaching a child in that range at home, outside a charter or PSP, files a PSA every year (CDE PSA FAQ). Our kids are 3 and under, so we don't file yet. Preschool-age learning at home needs no filing.

Step by step

  1. Withdraw from public school if your child is enrolled. Legally, this is a transfer to a private school.
  2. File the PSA online at www3.cde.ca.gov/psa. The statutory filing period is October 1–15, but the system is open August 1 to June 30 for new schools (CDE PSA information). You have to finish the form in one sitting.
  3. Teach the required subjects in English. These are the courses of study in Education Code 51210 and 51220 (CDE Schooling at Home FAQ).
  4. Keep records: attendance, courses of study, and the names, addresses and qualifications of faculty (CDE Schooling at Home). Use our free attendance log.

Things people often get wrong

  • The PSA is a notice, not an approval. The state does not license or endorse your school.
  • PSAs are public documents.
  • The CDE does not give curriculum advice. Its content standards are an optional guide.
