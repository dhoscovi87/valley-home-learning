How to hire a nanny or tutor safely and legally in California (2026)

1. Check backgrounds the official way

TrustLine is California's registry for in-home childcare providers. It's run by the CA Department of Social Services and includes California DOJ and FBI fingerprint checks plus the Child Abuse Central Index (TrustLine, CDSS In-Home Care). Parents can call 1-800-822-8490 to check whether a provider is registered. Agencies are required to use TrustLine. Families hiring directly are not, but it's the strongest check available. A provider pays about $143, one time.

2. Pay at least minimum wage

The California minimum wage is $16.90 an hour from January 1, 2026 (DIR MW-2026). Tracy has no higher local rate that we found. If your nanny works in a city with its own higher minimum, pay that rate.

3. Know the overtime rules (Domestic Worker Bill of Rights)

A nanny who mainly supervises, feeds and dresses children is a "personal attendant." They earn 1.5× pay for hours over 9 in a day or 45 in a week. If more than 20% of their time goes to other housework, regular overtime applies: over 8 hours a day or 40 a week (DIR FAQ). Casual babysitters and sitters under 18 are excluded.

4. Nanny taxes: California (EDD)

$750 or moreRegister with EDD (DE 1HW) within 15 days, and withhold SDI

Source: EDD 2026 Household Employer's Guide (DE 8829). If you pay $20,000 or less a year, you can choose to pay annually (DE 89).

5. Nanny taxes: federal (IRS)

You file Schedule H with your Form 1040 if you pay $1,000 or more in cash wages in any quarter (federal unemployment tax), or if you pay one employee wages at or above the Social Security and Medicare threshold for the year. Check the current threshold in IRS Publication 926 and the Schedule H instructions. You'll also issue a W-2.

6. Tutors: employee or contractor?

A tutor who runs their own business, sets their own methods, and works with several families may be an independent contractor. If you control how, when and where they work, California's ABC test usually makes them an employee. When in doubt, ask a professional.
